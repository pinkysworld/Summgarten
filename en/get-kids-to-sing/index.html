How to get kids to sing: playful ideas without pressure

Some children sing on their own, others need a reason. These ideas work without lessons and without any prior knowledge.

Listen first, then join in

Children usually sing when it feels safe. Sit next to your child and sing yourself, even if you do not think of yourself as a singer. Seeing adults hum shows that it is normal and fun.

Seven ideas for everyday life

  1. Hum instead of sing: One note on “mmm” is the lowest hurdle. Hum while getting dressed or brushing teeth.
  2. Echo game: You sing two or three notes, your child sings them back. Then swap.
  3. Animal sounds: Meowing, growling, tweeting: high and low notes appear on their own.
  4. Songs with movement: Clapping or swaying makes children forget they are singing.
  5. Little songs for routines: A tidy-up or goodnight song gives the day structure.
  6. Hear what your own voice does: Many children are amazed when a note comes back.
  7. No judging: Skip “right” and “wrong”. Praise for joining in is enough.

If your child does not want to sing

That is perfectly fine. Do not push or keep asking. Some children listen for weeks and then suddenly sing. Offer chances instead of asking for performance. If you have concerns about speech or hearing development, talk to your paediatrician; a game is no substitute for advice.

How a music game can help

Hum Garden (originally Summgarten) is a music game for ages 2–6 built around singing back: sing a sound and a little creature grows in the garden and sings it back. There is an echo part where the creatures answer, and a garden concert to listen to. The game has no scores and no time pressure.

The microphone is optional; without one there are 12 built-in sounds. Recordings stay on the device and can be deleted in the parents' area. Hum Garden is not a music course and does not replace lessons; it invites children to try things out. More on the music app for kids page.

FAQ

From what age can children sing along?

It varies. Hum Garden is designed for ages 2–6; many children hum or babble along early.

My child does not want to sing. What can I do?

Avoid pressure, sing yourself and offer opportunities. Many children join in later on their own.

Do I need a microphone for Hum Garden?

No. There are 12 built-in sounds; with a microphone your child can use their own voice.
